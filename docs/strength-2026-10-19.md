gameFull contained only 55 plies after the bot had already posted a6xb5 as
ply 56. The old full-state handler kept the newer board but inferred the turn

White then played Rb1 at ply 57; the bot never replied and lost on time.
The last server clock still showed 75.57 seconds for Black. The completed
export is preserved in `testdata/lichess_2026_10_19_reconnect_game.json`.

The same event sequence contains two roughly 60-second ponder waits. The
old watcher sets the ordinary abort flag once, but search startup and each
root depth reset that flag. Cancellation arriving before either reset can
therefore be lost until the ponder's 60-second time budget expires.

The candidate ignores gameStart for the current active game and full-state
snapshots older than the applied move count. Pondering now uses context
cancellation, which remains visible across ordinary abort resets, and a
cancelled board stream can stop while its event send is blocked. Existing
search and non-context callers retain their API. No search pruning or
static evaluation changes are part of this fix.

The two reconnect regressions fail on the deployed source and pass after the
patch. New tests cover cancellation before and during startup, explicit abort
resets, ten immediate real-ABDADA ponder start/stop cycles, and a cancelled
board stream blocked on delivery. An exact replay of the recorded 57 plies

## Contact-pressure evaluation experiment

The proposed static-scoring mechanism is confirmed. In the exact g5 position,
White's king-danger term is 1823 middlegame units both before and after Rh1,
although Rh1 relatively pins Bh2 to Qh6. This is approximately 885 cp at the
position's full middlegame phase. Disabling only Bh2's contact bonus reduces
that term to 1283 units, approximately 623 cp. The baseline static Black score
moves only from +792 after g5 to +749 after Rh1. The contact bonus therefore
ignores a concrete restraint on the attacker.

A local evaluation candidate excludes a minor's bespoke contact bonus when
an opposing slider pins it to its queen. Existing precomputed queen attacks
identify the clear line to the minor; a short ray checks the pinner. Extra
blockers, friendly sliders, wrong slider directions, and a minor able to
capture the pinner do not qualify. Ordinary attack maps and all evaluation
weights remain unchanged. After Rh1, this candidate scores Black +487,
262 cp below baseline; before the pin appears, scores remain unchanged.

Three alternating actual-budget replays select Re6 in every candidate run
and g5 in every deployed-baseline run. Candidate depths are five, six, five;
baseline depths are all five. Separate cold-hash Stockfish depth-22 restricted
searches score Re6 +54 and g5 -142; Nf6 scores +174. These finite-depth labels
support avoiding g5 without claiming Re6 is the strongest available move.
Existing Bxg6 attack, rook attack, knight retreat, and bishop defense gates

White last acknowledges Qb2 at ply 41 with 131.82 seconds remaining, then

The delivery fix keeps a locally applied reply pending until acknowledged.
Transport failures, partial bodies, HTTP 429, and 5xx responses request a fresh
board stream after a delay. Server history either confirms the exact UCI
reply or permits retrying that same selected move without another search.
A retry racing an already accepted move waits for authoritative confirmation.
Older snapshots do not rewind the board. Definitive illegal-move responses
still discard the desynced game. Draw offers survive retries and are encoded
as an actual URL query; response bodies close on every response path.
